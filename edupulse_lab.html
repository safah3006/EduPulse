01 · THE PROJECT

A practical student-support use case

This project turns a classroom question into two machine-learning tasks, then compares models on held-out students—not on the data they learned from.

PROJECT BRIEF
◎

Application

Use historical student information to estimate academic outcomes and surface possible support needs early. Predictions are exploratory signals for educators—not decisions about students.

↗

Two prediction tasks

Regression: estimate a final grade on the 0–20 scale. Classification: estimate the probability of passing, defined here as a final grade of 10 or higher.

⌁

What is compared

Linear Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest and KNN for numeric grades; Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest and KNN for pass/fail.

Why two kinds of models? Logistic Regression predicts a binary outcome (pass or not pass), while Linear Regression predicts a numeric grade. They answer different questions, so they are compared within their own task—not ranked against each other.

READ THE RESULTS
REGRESSION · GRADE ESTIMATION

How close are the predictions?

MAE is the average absolute error in grade points. RMSE also uses grade points, but penalizes large misses more. R² describes the variation explained relative to predicting the mean; higher is better and it may be negative.

Use RMSE as the primary comparison; also check MAE and R².

CLASSIFICATION · PASS LIKELIHOOD

Can the model identify both outcomes?

Precision is the share of predicted passes that pass. Recall is the share of actual passes found. F1 balances precision and recall; accuracy is the overall share correct. Check the confusion matrix and class balance, too.

Use F1 as the primary comparison so neither precision nor recall is ignored.

MODEL CHOICE

Why use Logistic Regression?

Logistic Regression is a classification model: it estimates a probability for a binary label. It belongs in the pass/fail comparison, not the numeric-grade comparison. Linear Regression is used for the numeric grade task.

The highlighted winner is the strongest on the current held-out split and selected primary metric—not a universally best model.

LIMITATIONS · RESPONSIBLE USE

What this project cannot tell us

The sample is small and specific to secondary-school mathematics in Portugal. Grades G1/G2 are earlier-period grades, so the demo assumes they are available. Correlation is not causation; these models cannot explain why a student is struggling or replace educator judgement.

Results can vary by split, threshold, features and population. Validate on new data before real-world use.
